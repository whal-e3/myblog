BEHIND THE MAP

A network, not a tunnel survey.

Seoul Subsurface turns geographic rail into an explorable three-dimensional map, with national rail context and Seoul K-pop places.

What you are looking at

The opening view shows South Korea and its passenger-rail corridors, then moves into the Seoul-area subway network. The South Korea button returns to that national view. The detailed Seoul network contains all lines, stations and links in its bundled August 24, 2026 snapshot. The separate national layer maps 12 operating regional metro/light-rail routes and 21 selected intercity physical corridors from OpenStreetMap, checked against operator route maps. Overlapping KTX, SRT, ITX and conventional services are not drawn as duplicate tracks. Jeongseon tourist rail is included; several short or unverified links, closed lines, and planned or unfinished lines are omitted. Mapped geometry is not a current timetable or a guarantee that every passenger service or station is present.

Seoul K-pop places are an editorial selection of publicly viewable landmarks. Click a location marker, zoom in to see an exterior-inspired 3D sketch, or choose a place in the sidebar for visitor notes and an official source. Marker positions, dimensions and architectural details are approximate, not surveyed replicas or access promises.

Height is a visualization device. Routes have slight vertical offsets so overlaps are easier to read. These offsets do not describe actual tunnel depths, elevation, station interiors, underground floors or construction geometry. Four-car 3D trains are generic models, not exact fleet replicas. Their identities, counts, positions, directions and movement are simulated. No displayed train represents a verified operating vehicle.

The source’s mapped path is retained for 842 segments. One Suin–Bundang segment has no source path and uses a straight station-to-station connector, identified in the downloadable data. Segment endpoints are aligned to the source station centers. Route colors use the source network palette. The light/dark blue interface is Seoul Metro–inspired and is not an official transit product. English names without a curated alias are automatic transliterations, not official signage.

Simulated trains · not live

The animated trains are invented on the 24 mapped Seoul-area routes. Their counts, identifiers, positions, directions and timing are a visual scenario, not current TOPIS observations, GPS tracks, a timetable, or arrival predictions. Motion is continuous so the 3D models look like trains traveling between mapped stations; it does not tell you where a real train is. Regional and intercity rail remains static context.

One afternoon sample of TOPIS report counts from October 14, 2026 provides a rough scale for 19 routes. A single snapshot cannot establish a usual timetable or fleet size. Counts and timing at other times, and on the five routes without a verified position feed, are illustrative assumptions. The animation runs without polling the position API. No API key is sent to the browser.

TRAIN SIMULATION / 가상 열차

These trains are invented.

The moving models show a plausible visual scenario on mapped rails. They are not live vehicles, provider reports or arrival predictions.

The simulation invents train counts and positions for the 24 mapped Seoul-area routes. One October 14, 2026 afternoon report-count sample gives a rough scale for 19 routes; other times and five routes use illustrative assumptions. The national rail layer has no moving trains.

What the 3D trains represent

Generic four-car models moving between mapped stations. A station name in the train inspector is a nearby map reference, not a current observation. The shown route, direction and endpoint describe the invented scenario. The position and timing should never be used for a trip decision.

No live feed is requested by the animation.

Optional source diagnostics

Seoul TOPIS provides station-level reports, not continuous GPS. The single afternoon sample is not a database of usual service. This animation does not display or continuously fetch provider reports; server-side feed diagnostics, when available, are separate from simulated motion.
